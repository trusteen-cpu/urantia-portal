The Urantia Revelation · 06

THE MELCHIZEDEK GOSPEL OF SALVATION

The first proclamation of salvation by faith — 1,973 BC (93:4.5 / 93:6.4)

This covenant of Melchizedek with Abraham represents the great Urantian agreement between divinity and humanity whereby God agrees to do everything; man only agrees to believe God's promises and follow his instructions.

The Urantia Book, 93:6.4
The Revolutionary Message

Melchizedek proclaimed that salvation — divine favor — is a FREE GIFT to all who believe, not earned through sacrifice, priestly mediation, or ritual. 'Good news' of radical grace.

Global Missionary Reach

Salem missionaries spread this gospel throughout Asia, India, China, and the Levant. Every major civilization received this teaching, though often later distorted (94:5.1; 95:2.5).

The Salem Creed (93:4.8)

Only two articles: (1) Believe in God the Father and maker of all things. (2) 'You shall not doubt that faith is the only requirement for eternal salvation.' Faith alone — nothing else.

Abraham's Response

Abraham 'believed halfheartedly, and even that was counted for righteousness' (93:4.5). Even imperfect faith receives the gift — grace is not conditional on spiritual perfection.

The Persistent Reversion

Each time this gospel was proclaimed, people reverted to sacrifice: 'The Semitic tribesmen preferred to go back to the older sacrifices' (93:6.4). Grace is always the hardest truth.

Gautama's Near-Miss

Siddhartha came 'surprisingly near to being a revival of the Salem gospel' (94:7.7) — proclaiming free salvation — but lost sight of the personal Father-God and thus the foundation.

The Urantia Revelation · 11

GRACE AND THE FREE GIFT OF SALVATION

Salvation as unconditional divine bestowal — Papers 150, 167, 169
The Parable of the Prodigal Son — The Supreme Illustration of Grace (169:1)

Jesus told the parable of the prodigal son to illustrate the unconditional love and grace of God toward erring children. The father runs to meet the returning son — not waiting for explanation or proof of reform — and immediately restores him to full sonship. This is the grace model of salvation: the initiative of the Father, the free restoration of status, and joy at the return.

169:1.4 also presents the lost coin: 'there is always joy in the presence of the angels of heaven over one sinner who repents.' God actively searches for the lost — the sheep that wandered, the coin covered by the dust of time.

Unconditional Offer 139:12.7

'The door of eternal life is wide open to all; whosoever will may come; there are no restrictions or qualifications save the faith of the one who comes.'

God's Initiative 169:1.4

The Father himself goes out to seek the lost sheep. Salvation is not merely available — it is actively pursued by a God who runs to meet the returning child.

Freely Received 150:5.2

'Entrance into the Father's kingdom is wholly free.' The only price is sincere desire and wholehearted faith — nothing more.

Grace With Limits 146:2.5

'Even the infinite love of God cannot force the salvation of eternal survival upon any mortal creature who does not choose to survive.' Grace never coerces.

The Terms 167:5.1

Jesus: 'The Father gives salvation to the children of men, and this salvation is a free gift to all who have the faith to receive sonship in the divine family.'

Active Acceptance

Grace is free but not automatic. The soul must choose. Love that compels is not love. The Father offers everything and forces nothing — freedom is honored.
